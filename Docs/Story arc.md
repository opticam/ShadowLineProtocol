# Story arc

The campaign opens with a guided run of milestones in Era 1, then opens up so the player can pursue any victory, thread or relationship, while a story director adapts events to what they do.

## At a glance

&#91;embedded content: campaign arc · 3 eras, about 60 turns\]

Era 1's milestones run in order; Era 2's threads arrive in any order the director chooses; Era 3 pulls them together.

## Era 1 — Landfall (turns 1–20)

Era 1 is a guided sequence of milestones. Each one introduces a system through a story mission, so the player learns by doing. Turn numbers are targets; milestones fire when their conditions are met.

| # | Milestone | Approx. turn | Story mission | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | **Manifest** | Before landing | Pack the first launch and the return flight; pick a starting partner from 3 | Mass budget, colony choice |
| 1 | **Landfall** | 1 | A cargo pod bounced off course on landing. Recover it before the first night. | EVA, movement, dose, placing a habitat, solar array and comms tower |
| 2 | **First Night** | 1–2 | Keep everyone alive through the first lunar night on stored power | Power, thermal, the day/night cycle |
| 3 | **First Contact** | 3–5 | Your partner colony's ice drill fails. Fix it on a joint mission. | Joint missions, commanding partner members, trade |
| 4 | **First Robot** | 4–6 | Assemble and field-test your first rover | Robots, command range, standing orders |
| 5 | **Solar Storm** | 6–8 | A solar particle event hits while a team is outside. Get them to shelter. | Bleed-out and rescue, stress, the radiation research line |
| 6 | **Mayday** | 9–11 | An Acquaintance-level colony's lander crashes. Rescue the survivors, then decide whether to shelter them. | Rescue, sheltering, relationship drivers |
| 7 | **Dark Tower** | 12–15 | A comms tower goes dark. The investigation finds it was sabotaged, not broken. | Investigation, clues, the central AI's first insight. Seeds the antagonist mystery without naming anyone. |
| 8 | **Sponsor Review** | 18–20 | Earth reviews your funding. Show oxygen production online, or lose launch priority. | Launch economics, self-sufficiency. Branches into Era 2. |

The Sponsor Review is the Era 1 crisis. Passing keeps your launch priority. Failing doesn't end the game: it forces you to lean on allies and trade, and it opens an independence storyline earlier.

## How the game opens up

After the Sponsor Review, there are no more fixed milestones. Missions come from six sources, and the player chooses which to take on. Unanswered missions have consequences.

| Mission source | Triggered by | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Antagonist thread | The director, timed to hurt | Sabotage investigations, clue hunts, confrontations, unmaskings |
| Research chains | Major research choices | Ice mining leads to a water-rights dispute; AI autonomy leads to a network-trust chain; deep drilling opens the lava tubes |
| Colony events | Other colonies' state | Shortages, rebellions, desperate trades, outlaw raids, refugees |
| Relationships | Friend and Ally bonds, crew bonds | Personal quests for your crew and for key members of other colonies |
| Environment | The Moon itself | Solar storms, moonquakes, micrometeoroid strikes, long nights |
| Exploration | Expansion and survey | Relay placement, sample collection, scouting new sites |

**Failure branches instead of ending.** A failed mission changes the story: a colony you couldn't save becomes refugees, or turns outlaw, or never forgives you.

## The story director

A story director, similar in spirit to RimWorld's storytellers, picks the next events each turn from the player's state so tension rises and falls instead of piling up.

**What it reads:** relationship levels, research progress, crew stress and injuries, resources and power, territory, each colony's stability, and how close anyone is to a victory.

**What it does**

- **Paces tension.** After a hard run, it gives a breather. After a quiet stretch, it raises the stakes.
- **Times the antagonists.** Antagonists act when it hurts most: a trade sours right before you need it, or a joint mission is sabotaged just as you near Ally level.
- **Reacts to your path.** A player chasing Expansion draws territorial disputes and raids. A Diplomatic player gets more politics and betrayals. A Shared-survival player meets colonies desperate for tech.
- **Scales with difficulty.** More colonies and higher difficulty mean more simultaneous events and sharper antagonists.
- **Keeps clues flowing.** Evidence surfaces steadily, so the mystery always moves forward without ever solving itself.

## Era 2 — Expansion & politics (turns 21–40)

Era 2 is open play. These story threads are available, and the director brings them in based on the player's choices and the state of the colonies. Most playthroughs will see several, in any order.

- **Independence:** a colony rebels against its sponsors. Back them, mediate, or side with Earth.
- **Desperation:** a struggling colony offers lopsided trades, then turns to theft or violence if no one helps.
- **The first outlaws:** a colony turns outlaw, opening the Liberation path.
- **The network:** networking your robots to the central AI makes the network an antagonist target.
- **The tubes:** deep drilling or survey opens the lava tubes, a new shelter and a deeper mystery.
- **Launch competition:** colonies vie for space on each launch, and deals for cargo capacity become political.
- **First unmasking:** clues converge on the first antagonist, so an unmasking is possible mid-era.
- **Crew arcs:** personal quests unlock as your crew grows, including relationships that can lead to marriage and family.

**Era 2 crisis** (the director picks one from the game state):

- **The Great Storm:** a major solar storm tests every shelter and colony at once.
- **The Blackout:** a coordinated attack on comms and robot networks.
- **Supply War:** a launch fails, and colonies fight over what's left.

## Era 3 — Convergence (turns 41–60)

In Era 3, the threads come together and the race for victory begins.

- **Rivals race too.** Other colonies pursue their own goals, so the player feels the pressure of time.
- **Antagonists escalate.** Any remaining antagonists make their boldest moves, often aimed at whatever the player relies on most.
- **Final crisis.** The director builds the last crisis from unresolved threads: an unmasked antagonist's allies, an outlaw coalition, a failing colony, or a sponsor recall.

**Endings.** Each victory has its own ending, followed by an epilogue shaped by how you played: your crew's fates, families started, colonies saved or lost, and antagonists reformed.

**Failure states**

- The colony can no longer sustain itself, for example after losing key crew early.
- Every crew member is lost.
- Turn 60 arrives with no victory. This counts as survival without a win, and gets a bittersweet epilogue.
